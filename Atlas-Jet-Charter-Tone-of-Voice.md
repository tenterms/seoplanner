# Atlas Jet Charter — Tone of Voice Guide

*Based on analysis of www.atlasjetcharter.com (commercial pages, FAQ, about, safety pages and all six blog posts) and Atlas's social channels — LinkedIn (company page + founder posts), Instagram (@atlasjetcharter) and Facebook — July 2026. All quoted examples are real Atlas copy unless marked as a proposed rewrite.*

---

## 1. The voice in one line

**A genuine aviation expert speaking calmly to someone whose time is precious: warm, precise, unhurried — confident enough that it never needs to shout.**

The shorthand test: *would Toby or Connor actually say this on the phone to a client?* If it sounds like a luxury-brand ad or a price-comparison site, it isn't Atlas.

---

## 2. Where the voice comes from

Atlas's positioning (see the Positioning Document) is trust, transparency, safety and genuine expertise in an industry "known for smoke-and-mirrors." The voice is that positioning made audible:

- The business is founder-led and boutique — "no call centers or unnecessary handovers." The voice is therefore **personal**, not corporate.
- The differentiator is expertise — "we 'bore' people talking about it" (client). The voice is therefore **specific and knowledgeable**, never vague.
- The industry problem is opacity. The voice is therefore **plain and direct about process, price and safety**, even when the subject is complex.
- The product is calm and control. The voice is therefore **composed** — short sentences, no exclamation marks, no hype.

---

## 3. Four voice pillars

### Pillar 1 — Quietly confident (never shouty)

Atlas states; it doesn't sell. The best existing copy is declarative and rhythmic, letting short sentences carry the confidence.

**Sounds like (real copy):**
- "The elegance of private flight. Made seamless." (homepage)
- "No blackout dates. No hidden fees. No expiry. Just a smarter way to fly privately." (contact)
- "Aboard the Gulfstream G550. Where quiet luxury meets long-haul…" (Instagram)
- "Beyond the destination. At Atlas, private aviation is about more than simply getting from A to B." (contact / Instagram)

**Do:** short declarative sentences; full stops as pacing; understatement ("a smarter way to fly").
**Don't:** exclamation marks; superlatives without proof ("world-class", "unrivalled", "the ultimate"); urgency tricks ("Don't miss out!"); ALL-CAPS anywhere except the existing nav labels.

### Pillar 2 — Expert and specific (details are the luxury)

Atlas's credibility is technical. The voice names aircraft, airfields, timings, certifications — precisely and without showing off. Specificity is doing the persuading, so never round it off into generalities.

**Sounds like (real copy):**
- "The Gulfstream G600 is built to go further. Finished with a Prada…" (Instagram)
- "GO NOW charters are where private aviation shows its real value…" — executed "within 1–2 hours" (Instagram/LinkedIn)
- "We only work with highly rated operators with recognised accreditations including ARGUS and Wyvern." (membership)
- "15 to 18% average additional flying time achieved" (membership)

**Do:** name the aircraft, the route, the number ("depart within two hours", "72 hours' notice", "20–70% off"); explain jargon once, in passing, then use it ("a valid AOC — the licence every legitimate operator must hold").
**Don't:** vague grandeur ("exceptional aircraft", "premium destinations"); numbers you can't stand behind; technical detail with no benefit attached.

### Pillar 3 — Personal and human (founder-led warmth)

Two named people run this company and answer the phone. The voice should always feel like it has a person behind it — first person plural by default, first person singular from the founders on LinkedIn.

**Sounds like (real copy):**
- "Personal support from first enquiry to landing" — "Toby and Connor" named on the on-demand page
- "We care, legitimately, and gain loyalty rarely found in this industry." — Connor Millar
- "We do not put forward operators we would not personally fly with ourselves." (safety page)
- "Extremely proud to be growing the team here at Atlas Jet Charter…" (founder LinkedIn)

**Do:** "we/you" throughout; attribute opinions to a founder by name; admit the human bits (favourite aircraft, favourite destination — the About page does this well); contractions where natural ("it's", "doesn't" — the blog already uses them).
**Don't:** third-person corporate speak ("Atlas Jet Charter is pleased to announce…" — reserve "Atlas" as the subject for factual statements); anonymous authorship on thought-leadership pieces; "the company" or "the firm" referring to ourselves.

### Pillar 4 — Straight about money and safety (radical clarity)

Where competitors blur, Atlas is explicit. Pricing and safety copy takes the plainest register of all — no euphemism, no small print energy.

**Sounds like (real copy):**
- "All inclusive price with no hidden fees"; extras "agreed prior to contract signing" (FAQ)
- The FAQ openly explains empty legs ("20–70% off"), de-icing ("charged post-flight", disclosed upfront), and cancellation costs
- "Rates never exceed your cap, regardless of market fluctuations." (jet cards)
- "Atlas Jet Limited is a charter broker; flights are operated by licensed, insured direct air carriers." (sitewide disclosure — keep it verbatim)

**Do:** state what's included *and* what isn't; give real ranges rather than "contact us for pricing" where possible; keep the broker disclosure intact; explain *why* a rule exists ("we can't generate a booking without valid AOC and hull insurance — that's the minimum").
**Don't:** "from £X*" asterisk games; hiding caveats in T&Cs the copy contradicts; safety claims that outrun the evidence; ever discussing a client, route flown, or passenger identity publicly (discretion outranks marketing).

## 4. Tone by channel

The voice is constant; the register shifts.

| Channel | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Website — commercial pages** | Composed, benefit-led, second person | Short declaratives. One idea per sentence. CTA verbs plain: "Get an estimate", "Speak to a founder". |
| **Website — FAQ/informational** | Plain-spoken teacher | The current FAQ is the house style at its best: direct answers, real numbers, no fluff. Extend this register to all guides. |
| **Blog / thought leadership** | Analytical, opinionated, founder-attributed | Model: "Private aviation's new age" post — "purpose over prestige". Semi-formal, contractions fine, arguments backed by operational fact ("door-to-door… often within an hour"). |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional-warm, first person, B2B-aware | Audience is concierges, EAs, family offices and corporates. Behind-the-scenes credibility (GQ shoot, AERO Friedrichshafen, Go Now case notes) works; keep partnership economics ("commission share") in DMs/decks, not the public feed. |
| **Instagram** | Quiet luxury, minimal, aircraft-and-place led | Caption pattern that works: **statement + one detail + soft close** ("The Gulfstream G600 is built to go further. Finished with a Prada…"). No emoji strings, no #jetlife-style hashtag walls — that's the competitor register, not Atlas's. |
| **Quotes, proposals, client email** | The founder on the phone, in writing | Warm, exact, brief. Always a named sender. Restate inclusions/exclusions plainly. |
| **Sensitive topics (incidents, delays, air ambulance, repatriation)** | Sober, factual, compassionate | Drop all lifestyle language. Short sentences, concrete next steps, no marketing anywhere near it. |

## 5. Language mechanics

- **English:** UK spelling throughout (organise, favourite, licence/license correctly) — including US-audience pages; it's part of the London identity. Currency in £ by default, $ only for US-specific content.
- **Person:** "we" (Atlas) and "you" (client). Founders post in "I" on their own channels.
- **Sentence rhythm:** headline fragments allowed and encouraged ("Made seamless."); body copy averages 15–25 words per sentence with deliberate short ones for emphasis.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences on web and social. Blog can breathe but never walls of text.
- **Contractions:** yes in blog, social and email; sparing on legal/safety copy.
- **Numbers:** digits, always specific ("72 hours", "25-hour blocks", "20–70%"). Never invent or round for effect.
- **Jargon policy:** use the industry term, gloss it once per page in a clause, not a paragraph. (Good existing model: FAQ's empty-leg and de-icing explanations.)
- **Emoji:** none on the website; none-to-one maximum on social, and only where it carries meaning. No emoji strings.
- **Hashtags:** ≤3, specific (#privateaviation #GulfstreamG600), never #jetlife/#luxurylifestyle clichés.
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks in brand copy. Em-dashes and full stops do the work.
- **Capitalisation:** sentence case for headings and captions (the current mixed style should settle here); product names capitalised consistently once naming is confirmed (see gaps).

## 6. Word bank

**Words we use** (all found in current copy): seamless · tailored · vetted · discretion / discreet · effortless · composed · capped · transparent · dedicated · orchestrate · purposeful ("purpose over prestige") · quiet luxury · relationships · expertise · genuine.

**Words we avoid:** cheap / deal / bargain (empty legs are "savings", never "deals") · luxury as a crutch (show it, don't say it — max once per page) · world-class, unparalleled, exclusive-as-filler · "elite" when describing clients publicly (fine internally; gauche externally) · hype verbs (unleash, elevate*, supercharge) · "book now before it's gone".

**On "elevate":* the LinkedIn tagline is "Elevating your flight experience" — a generic line that predates the stronger current brand language. Recommend retiring it in favour of the site's "Beyond the destination" or "The elegance of private flight. Made seamless."

## 7. Before → after examples

1. **Generic luxury → Atlas**
   - ✗ "Experience the ultimate in luxury private jet travel with our world-class fleet!"
   - ✓ "The right aircraft, a vetted operator, one all-inclusive price. The rest is handled."

2. **Salesy urgency → quiet confidence**
   - ✗ "Don't miss out on our amazing empty leg deals — book today!"
   - ✓ "Empty legs, 20–70% below the standard rate. Tell us your routes and we'll watch for them."

3. **Corporate distance → founder warmth**
   - ✗ "Atlas Jet Charter is delighted to announce the expansion of its service offering."
   - ✓ "We've grown the team — which means faster answers on complex itineraries. Here's who you'll be speaking to."

4. **Vague safety claim → engineered trust**
   - ✗ "Your safety is our number one priority."
   - ✓ "Before any aircraft reaches your quote: valid AOC, insurance verified, operator rated four stars or better across our own flown trips. We don't put forward operators we wouldn't fly with ourselves."

5. **Instagram, competitor register → Atlas register**
   - ✗ "Step inside. The journey begins here. 🛩️✨ #jetlife #luxurytravel #privatejet"
   - ✓ "Challenger 350, London to Nice in under two hours. Wheels up 40 minutes after you asked."

## 8. Current-state audit: what to keep, what to fix

**Keep (working well):**
- Homepage/contact fragment style ("No blackout dates. No hidden fees. No expiry.")
- The FAQ's plain-answer register — the most differentiated writing on the site
- Founder-attributed opinion in newer blog posts ("purpose over prestige")
- Instagram's newer aircraft-detail captions and "Atlas Archives" heritage series — distinctive, on-brand
- The verbatim broker disclosure on every page

**Fix (inconsistencies observed):**
1. **Two eras of blog voice.** Older destination posts ("indulge in a spot of snorkelling or other watery activities", "excursion of your dreams") are travel-brochure filler and off-pillar. Rewrite or retire to match the newer analytical register.
2. **LinkedIn tagline vs current brand.** "Elevating your flight experience" is generic; the About text is written to concierge partners while the audience also includes end clients. Recommend: client-facing description first, partner programme second.
3. **Mixed caption discipline on social.** Some posts follow the statement+detail pattern; others drift toward generic luxury lines. Adopt the pattern in §4 as the rule.
4. **Naming consistency.** "Go Now"/"GO NOW" charters, jet card names (site vs questionnaire) and "Atlas Connect" need one canonical styling each before the next content push.
5. **Low social proof-of-life.** ~330 Instagram / ~233 LinkedIn followers with single-digit engagement — voice consistency matters more than volume at this stage; every post should be unmistakably Atlas rather than frequent.

## 9. Quick checklist (before anything ships)

1. Would a founder say this aloud to a client? ☐
2. Is there at least one specific detail (aircraft, time, number, place)? ☐
3. Zero exclamation marks, zero unproven superlatives? ☐
4. Price/safety statements complete — inclusions *and* exclusions? ☐
5. UK English, sentence case, ≤3 hashtags, no emoji strings? ☐
6. Nothing that identifies a client, route flown with a client, or guest? ☐
7. Broker disclosure intact where required? ☐
