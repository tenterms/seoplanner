## 3. What they do and who they sell to

BOM IT Solutions is one of Bristol's oldest technology businesses, founded 1958 as Bristol Office Machines. Services: 24/7 IT support, managed IT services, cyber security (Cyber Essentials Plus and ISO 27001 certified), cloud and data centre (Azure, private cloud, dedicated servers), Microsoft 365/Copilot ("Modern Working"), connectivity, and a newer "Applied AI" line. They sell B2B to organisations "of any size"; visible clients skew mid-market and larger: Nisbets, Trading 212, LKAB Minerals, The Trade Centre, Turtle Bay, Thomas & Thomas Solicitors, plus charities and schools. Certified B Corporation and Living Wage employer, which is genuine differentiation in the MSP market and useful AI-answer material.

Search-relevant notes: demand is local-commercial ("it support bristol" and neighbouring towns) plus national service terms; no meaningful seasonality; many service lines means real risk of thin coverage, and the current site's marketing brand ("bit58", "Flex As You Grow") is invisible to search demand phrasing.

## 6. Search and AI visibility snapshot

*Spot check, 7 August 2026: Ahrefs (GB) plus a live localised Google pull. Not a full audit.*

**The headline: the site is organically invisible, and it is recent, self-inflicted, technical damage.**

- Ahrefs currently shows **1 ranking keyword** for bomitsolutions.co.uk in GB: their own brand name. Estimated organic traffic: ~1 visit/month. bom.co.uk (their email domain) carries nothing either.
- It was not always this bad. In March 2026 they ranked top-5 for "it company bristol" (200/mo), "bristol it company" (100/mo), position 1 for "grc cyber security" (300/mo), plus top-20 spots for "small business it services", "sme it support", "zero trust network access" and others. Organic traffic ran at a modest but real 100–220 visits/month from 2024 through March 2026.
- Then: **132 visits in March → 60 in April → 15 in May → 4 in June → 1 in August 2026.** A collapse to zero over four months.
- Cause, verified live: the site has been rebuilt (new WordPress build on WP Engine, new "What we do / Who we help / bit58" architecture) and the old URL structure was not properly redirected. Their old managed IT services page (`/it-support-and-services/managed-it-services/`, still surfacing in Google) now **301-redirects to an SVG image file** (`/wp-content/uploads/managed-it-services.svg`). `/news/` returns a 404. Robots and meta robots are clean, so this is a broken redirect map, not a noindex accident. Domain Rating is 16 (weak but workable for local terms).

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

Basis: sector norms for managed IT (£10k–£40k/year per client, multi-year retention), positioned mid-range because BOM's visible client base skews mid-market rather than 5-seat micro businesses. Assume **£15k–£25k/year per new managed client**, and note MSP clients commonly stay 3+ years, so first-year value understates lifetime value. Assumptions to replace with their real figures on the call.

The conservative case clears a £1,500–£2,500/month retainer (£18k–£30k/year) by roughly 3x, and a single retained client at the £20k+ end pays for most of a year. The maths works at standard tiers without stretching.

## 8. Size of the prize

Two distinct prizes, which is what makes this brief unusual:

1. **Stop the bleeding (immediate, provable).** The rebuild broke their URL graph and took even their modest prior visibility to zero. Fixing redirects and re-establishing the commercial pages is recovery of something they already owned, evidenced by their own decline curve. This is urgent: every month the broken state persists makes recovery slower.
2. **Win a market they have never held (the growth case).** ~1,400+ local commercial searches/month plus national SME terms, in a KD 1–6 market, against competitors whose main advantage is simply having pages and reviews. Even at peak, BOM captured only 100–220 visits/month; the market allows several times that. Conservative framing: capturing a top-3 position on the Bristol head terms alone plausibly yields 2–6 qualified enquiries/month, which is £75k–£480k/year on the table in section 7 terms. Their 68-year history, B Corp status and client logos are exactly the trust ingredients Google's map pack and AI answers reward, and they are currently unused.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

Strong fit: B2B, high-value recurring customers, a search-driven local market, and a website dramatically underperforming the quality of the business. 

**Suggested entry: a paid technical recovery and visibility review, fast, then the retainer.** The broken-rebuild evidence (traffic curve, SVG redirect, 404s) makes the review close to self-selling and positions TenTerms as the people who diagnosed what their web developers missed. Do not open by criticising the new site's design; the site is fresh and someone internal championed it. Frame as "the rebuild is done and looks good; the migration left broken redirects that have cost you your Google presence, here is the evidence, here is the fix." Then the £1,500–£2,500/month tier for the visibility build (service/location pages, reviews and map pack, the listicle/AI-answer ingredient work). One caution from experience: recovery needs URL stability after the fix, so the plan should explicitly freeze the URL graph once repaired.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. Walk me through the website rebuild: when did it go live, who built it, and what were you hoping it would do? (Dates the damage, sizes the sunk cost, and tells us who not to embarrass.)
2. Have you looked at Search Console since the spring? What did you make of the trend? (Tests whether the collapse is known internally; only they can see GSC.)
3. Where does new business come from today, and roughly what share starts with a web search or the website? (Baseline for the value chain.)
4. What is a typical managed services client worth to you in year one, and how long do they usually stay? (Replaces our £15k–£25k assumption with their number.)
5. Who looks after marketing day to day, and who would review and approve pages if we worked together? (Capacity test.)
6. If this stacked up, who besides you would want to see the numbers before going ahead? Would Tom or Clare want to be in that conversation? (Authority test, politely.)
7. Is the growth focus Bristol and the South West, or national? And where does bit58 fit in your plans? (Scopes the keyword map and the architecture.)
8. Your Google reviews are strong but Netitude and EvolvIT own the map pack with fewer credentials than yours. Is anyone actively asking clients for reviews? (Cheap early win; tests appetite for process.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **Decision authority is split.** MD Andy Carter runs the MSP; the owners run Clue Software. Marketing spend approval path unknown. Mitigate by getting an owner into conversation two.
- **"We just paid for a website."** The rebuild is recent; budget and pride may both be spent. The technical-recovery framing (fix the migration, not redo the site) is the way in. If they insist the site is fine as-is, the evidence pack settles it.
- **No visible marketing function.** Content review and approvals may bottleneck on busy operations people. Price and pace delivery accordingly.
- **Turnover unverified.** Filleted accounts; the £2.2m–£4.4m band is an estimate from headcount. FY2025 retained earnings dipped slightly; almost certainly dividends, but confirm the business is as comfortable as the balance sheet suggests.
- **Lead source unstated.** This research assumed a cold approach; if there is an existing relationship or referral route to Carter, Drohan or Elford, reachability improves and the entry framing should adjust.
- **Not establishable from here:** GSC data (manual actions, indexing state of the new URLs), the full redirect map, whether an SEO/web agency is already engaged, and live AI assistant answers (run 2–3 prompts before the call).
