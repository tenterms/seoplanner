## 3. What they do and who they sell to

Commercial HVAC is the core: boiler and plant maintenance, air conditioning, ventilation, plus electrical, refurbishment/fit-out and security (CCTV, access control). Revenue model is the classic FM mix: recurring PPM contracts, reactive call-outs (2-hour response, 24/7), and project work (boiler replacements, fit-outs). Customers are schools (150+ served annually per the site; "more than 50 schools" in an older description), care homes, hotels, restaurants, housing associations, property managers, and marquee sites including Westfield and Brent Cross shopping centres and David Lloyd clubs. Service area is London plus the M3/M4 home counties, which makes this a "[service] + [place]" search market with commercial intent spread across dozens of town and borough terms. They market an all-electric vehicle fleet and a zero-carbon-buildings angle, useful differentiation for content.

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

No pricing is published, so this uses sector norms, stated as assumptions. A commercial PPM contract for a school, care home or restaurant plant room typically runs £3k to £10k a year, and contract clients also generate reactive call-outs and project work (a commercial boiler replacement is £10k to £50k). A blended new commercial client is credibly worth **£5k to £8k in year one**, with FM retention commonly running three years plus (retention is upside, not in the headline numbers).

| Scenario | Extra enquiries/month | Close rate | New customers/year | Revenue impact (first 12 months per customer at £5k to £8k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2 | 1 in 5 | ~5 | £25k to £40k |
| Realistic | 4 | 1 in 4 | ~12 | £60k to £96k |
| Stretch | 6 | 1 in 3 | ~24 | £120k to £192k |

Against an £18k/year retainer (£1,500 a month), the conservative case returns 1.4x to 2.2x, below the 3x comfort line; the realistic case clears it at 3.3x to 5.3x. Conclusion: the standard retainer maths works here only at the lower tier, or the engagement should start smaller (see section 9). One caveat that helps them: a single school or shopping-centre PPM win at the top of the value range materially beats these averages.

## 8. Size of the prize

The identified commercial terms alone represent roughly 4,000 to 5,000 GB searches a month, before the "[service] [town]" long tail across six counties, in SERPs where most difficulty scores are under 20 and the winners are ordinary regional SMEs. Bonus Eventus currently captures approximately zero of it. Framings, each on the section 7 assumptions:

- **Conservative:** rank for a handful of low-difficulty PPM and county terms within 9 to 12 months; 2 extra enquiries a month; £25k to £40k of first-year client revenue, recurring thereafter.
- **Realistic:** own "commercial hvac maintenance", "planned preventative maintenance" and 10 to 15 town/service pages within 12 to 18 months; £60k to £96k a year of new recurring maintenance revenue.
- **Stretch:** become the review-backed default for commercial HVAC maintenance across Surrey/Hants/Berks plus AI-answer presence; £120k+ a year, plus project work riding on the contracts.

Because FM clients recur, each year's wins stack: the realistic case compounds to a £150k+ annual revenue difference by year three, on retention assumptions that are standard for the sector.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

Strong fit, wrong opening if pitched at the top tier. Recommended entry: **a paid visibility review** presenting the invisibility evidence (DR 3.5, three keywords, the listicle absence next to their 788 reviews), followed by **a £1,500 to £2,500 a month retainer** focused on: (1) a proper service-page architecture (five services by six counties, built selectively around demand), (2) consolidating the 400-post blog sprawl into a dozen strong guides, and (3) making their review corpus legible to search engines and AI answers. A full website rebuild conversation is likely unavoidable given the current structure, but position it as phase two, funded by early wins. Their zero-carbon/heat-pump angle is genuine content differentiation worth using.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. Roughly what share of new business currently comes from the website versus word of mouth and Checkatrade? (Tests the cost of doing nothing; the research suggests the website contributes almost nothing.)
2. What is a typical PPM contract worth to you annually, and how long does a client usually stay? (Replaces the £5k to £8k assumption with their number.)
3. Which would you rather win more of: PPM contracts, reactive call-outs, or refurb projects? And in which counties are you hungriest? (Directs the page architecture.)
4. You have over 700 five-star reviews across Checkatrade and Google; who looks after those platforms today? (Surfaces the marketing capacity gap and flatters accurately.)
5. If we drafted service pages, who on your team could review them for technical accuracy, and how quickly? (Tests delivery capacity; likely answer is Laszlo, whose time is scarce.)
6. Would you both want to see the numbers before going ahead, or does one of you lead on this kind of decision? (Tests decision process between the two owner-directors.)
7. Turnover isn't in your filed accounts; are we right to think you're in the £1m to £2m range? (Calibrates affordability honestly.)
8. Have you ever asked ChatGPT who the best commercial HVAC firm in Surrey is? (Sets up the AI visibility conversation.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **Affordability ceiling.** Disclosed profit is £71k; a top-tier retainer is out of range. If the turnover estimate is at the low end, even £2k a month needs a visible payback path. Enter small.
- **Capacity.** No marketing function; four office staff running a subcontractor network. Content approval will bottleneck on the owners unless the process is designed around minimal client time.
- **Website platform unknown.** The URL sprawl suggests posts auto-published from social media for years. The rebuild-versus-retrofit question needs a technical look before any proposal promises timelines.
- **Lead source unknown.** This briefing assumed cold. If there is an existing relationship or enquiry, reachability and entry point improve.
- **One visible negative review** (a boiler repair dispute totalling £1,684.80 in charges) amid hundreds of positives; immaterial, but be aware it exists if reviews come up.
- **Not established:** VAT registration, actual turnover, contract mix between PPM/reactive/projects, and whether "Bonus Eventus Oxon" has separate marketing ambitions.
