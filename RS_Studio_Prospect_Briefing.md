## 3. What they do and who they sell to

RS Studio was set up in 2019 (the brand; the company behind it dates to 2011) to provide "pragmatic, solutions-driven architecture in a caring, yet candid manner". Services run across:

- **Residential** (B2C): extensions, loft conversions, new builds, planning applications and building regulation packages. Projects visible in Southampton, Winchester and Andover.
- **Commercial** (B2B): mezzanine installations, warehouse solutions, and work with developers; a testimonial describes a 3-apartment scheme taken through planning and building control.
- **Community**: sports facility design for St Swithun's Girls' School; religious buildings are a listed specialty.

Positioning is local ("a focus on localism") and problem-led ("Bad architecture has long-term consequences"). New work today appears to come through referrals and networking: Shahi is an active member of BNI Premier Southampton and posts regularly on LinkedIn (planning appeal wins, project updates, Southampton planning statistics). Search strategy implication: demand is local-commercial ("architect southampton" cluster) plus a large national informational layer (fees, permitted development, RIBA stages) that they have already started writing for but that earns them nothing yet.

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

RS Studio publishes fees (rare and useful): RIBA Stages 1–3 at £1,450–£2,450 +VAT and Stage 4 at £1,000–£1,950 +VAT. A residential client taken through Stages 1–4 is therefore worth roughly **£2,500–£4,400**, with M&E plans, 3D imagery and contract administration extra. Commercial and multi-unit work (the 3-apartment scheme, warehouse projects) plausibly runs **£5k–£20k+** per project. Blended average assumption: **£3,500 per new client**, residential-weighted.

| Scenario | Extra enquiries/month | Close rate | New clients/year | Revenue impact (first 12 months) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2 | 1 in 4 | ~6 | ~£21,000 |
| Realistic | 4 | 1 in 4 | ~12 | ~£42,000 |
| Stretch | 6 | 1 in 3 | ~24 | ~£85,000+ (assumes some commercial wins lifting the average) |

Assumptions: their own published fees; a 25%–33% close rate on high-intent local enquiries, defensible for a 5.0-rated practice; no repeat-business multiplier (architecture is largely one-off per household, though developer clients repeat).

**The honest comparison:** the conservative case (£21k) does not clear 3x even the bottom retainer (£18k/year at £1,500/month). The realistic case clears it at just over 2x. This prospect does not support the standard retainer offer; it supports a smaller engagement where even the conservative case pays back several times over.

## 8. Size of the prize

The local cluster ("architect(s) (in) southampton" + Winchester + Hampshire, ~600 searches/month) plus national low-difficulty service terms represents realistic first-page potential of a few hundred visits/month within 6–12 months, against a current baseline of zero. Because the baseline is zero, every enquiry generated is incremental.

- **Conservative:** claim and fix the Google profile, fix the address conflict, add 6–8 service and location pages → 2 extra enquiries/month → **~£21k/year** in fees.
- **Realistic:** the above plus making the existing guides rank (they already target fees/permitted development/RIBA-stage demand) and directory/listicle placement → **~£42k/year**.
- **Stretch:** organic page-one for the Southampton cluster plus commercial enquiries (one warehouse or apartment scheme is worth 3–5 residential jobs) → **£85k+/year**.

Against estimated turnover of £250k–£450k, the realistic case is a 10%–15% revenue uplift from a channel currently contributing nothing.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

**Do not lead with a retainer.** The financial picture will not comfortably support £1,500+/month, and pitching it risks losing a winnable smaller deal. Suggested entry:

1. **Paid visibility review** as the opener: the "zero organic keywords, DR 0.8, unclaimed 36-review profile, two addresses" story is vivid, entirely factual, and demonstrable on a screen-share in five minutes.
2. **A fixed-price quick-wins project**: claim the Google Business Profile, resolve the NAP conflict, build the missing service pages (extensions, lofts, new builds, planning applications) and location pages (Southampton, Winchester, Eastleigh, Andover), retitle the existing guides, and secure the easy directory/listicle placements. Priced as a project, this fits a micro business and captures most of the prize, because the competition at this level is thin.
3. Revisit a small retainer once enquiries visibly move, or if discovery reveals commercial/developer revenue that changes the affordability picture.

There is also a genuine risk-framing angle: the practice's only search asset is an unclaimed Google listing with the wrong address. Anyone could suggest an edit to it; Google could re-source the data. Their one working channel is unmanaged.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. Roughly what share of new work currently comes from BNI and referrals versus people finding you online? (Tests the cost of doing nothing and channel concentration.)
2. What is a typical residential project worth to you through all stages, and how does a commercial or developer project compare? (Replaces our £3,500 blended assumption with their number.)
3. How many enquiries a month do you get now, and how many can the team actually absorb given you're hiring a project architect? (Tests capacity and sizes the target.)
4. The Google listing showing Fairways House with 36 five-star reviews, and the website showing Rockstone Place: which address is current, and does anyone in the practice have access to that Google profile? (Surfaces the NAP problem and the unmanaged-channel risk.)
5. Is RS Studio's contracting entity Sikhingit Limited, and does any work run through Horizon Property? (Confirms the legal entity and the true financial picture.)
6. If we produced draft service pages, who would review and approve them, and how quickly? (Tests client-side capacity; the honest answer is probably "Ranjeet, eventually".)
7. Would you want spend decisions of £X to be yours alone, or is there anyone else who'd want to see the numbers? (Confirms what the register suggests: he signs alone.)
8. The commercial side (mezzanines, warehouses, developer schemes): is that a segment you want more of? (One commercial win reframes the whole ROI conversation.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **Affordability is the central risk.** Micro-entity accounts, ~4 staff, estimated turnover £250k–£450k. Oversized proposals will die here.
- **Legal entity unconfirmed.** Sikhingit Limited is a high-confidence inference (SIC code, director, address), not a website-footer fact. Note also an unrelated namesake, RS STUDIO LTD (16020236, Cardiff, dissolved March 2026): do not confuse the two in any collateral.
- **Owner-bottleneck delivery risk.** One decision maker who is also the reviewer, the rainmaker and the lead architect. Content approval could stall; the quick-wins scope should minimise what he has to review.
- **Website ownership/state unknown.** Built by Studio 950, copyright notice still says 2020, and it's WordPress. CMS access and hosting control need confirming before promising page builds.
- **Address history.** Two addresses in circulation (Mount Pleasant Road on Google, Rockstone Place on the site) suggests an office move that was never propagated; there may be more stale citations elsewhere.
- Could not establish: actual enquiry volumes, revenue split between residential and commercial, whether the LinkedIn headcount of 7 reflects payroll or collaborators, and whether the practice has any marketing budget history.
