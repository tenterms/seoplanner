A well-reviewed, owner-run practice that is invisible for every Southampton commercial search and visible only for Totton. ## 3. What they do and who they sell to

The practice offers the full IFA range. Nick has advised since 1989 (Prudential, then St James's Place, independent since 2003) and specialises in pensions and investments. Lorna joined in 2017, holds the DipFA Level 4, and has built the mortgage and protection side, which is where the website and every Google review point.

Customers are consumers: first-time buyers, remortgagers, the self-employed, buy-to-let landlords, people approaching retirement, and families dealing with inheritance. The FAQ says most clients are in the south of England, with some UK-wide clients served remotely. This is a local-first business with two distinct demand pools:

- **Mortgages and protection** (Lorna). High volume, lower value per case, fiercely competitive in Southampton, heavily fee-free positioned by rivals.
- **Pensions, investments and estate planning** (Nick). Lower volume, much higher value per client, long retention, competition is more fragmented.

The practice sits inside the Protection & Investment group, which is the FCA principal. The group describes itself as a co-operative of independent practices. Each adviser practice appears to trade through its own company and be paid via the group; the group's own accounts show £1.3m of sales to and £1.0m of purchases from "other related parties" in the year to July 2025, which is consistent with adviser income flowing through head office. That structure matters because financial promotions (web pages, blog posts) will need the group's compliance sign-off.

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

No fees are published. Estimates by product line, stated as assumptions:

- **Mortgage client.** Lender procuration fee of about 0.35% to 0.4% on a typical Southampton loan of £200k to £250k gives £700 to £1,000, plus any broker fee (unknown, possibly nil), plus protection commission of £300 to £1,000 where life or income cover is sold alongside. First-year value £1,000 to £2,000. Repeat at remortgage every two to five years.
- **Pension or investment client.** Initial advice fee of 1% to 3% on invested funds plus ongoing 0.5% to 1% a year. On a £150k to £250k pot that is £2,500 to £6,000 in year one and £1,000 to £2,500 a year thereafter, with retention typically measured in years.
- **Blended first-year value: about £2,000**, assuming roughly seven mortgage enquiries for every three wealth enquiries, which matches the profile of the site and reviews.

Conversion chain, using first-year value only:

| Scenario | Extra enquiries/month | Close rate | New clients/year | First-year revenue impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2 | 1 in 4 | ~6 | £12,000 |
| Realistic | 4 | 1 in 3 | ~16 | £32,000 |
| Stretch | 6 | 1 in 3 | ~24 | £48,000 (more if the mix tilts to wealth clients) |

(Figures are the expected revenue from each new client over 12 months. Ongoing adviser fees and remortgage repeats add to this in later years but are excluded from the headline.)

The conservative case is below three times a £1,500 a month retainer (£18k a year), and only the realistic case clears it. That says the customer value is too low for the standard offer unless the wealth side is the target. A project priced in the low thousands clears the conservative case comfortably.

## 8. Size of the prize

About 1,500 commercial searches a month across Southampton, Hampshire, New Forest, Eastleigh and Romsey, plus Totton demand that does not register in keyword tools but shows up as map-pack traffic. A two-adviser practice will not take the Southampton mortgage head terms from The Mortgage Hut, and should not try. The winnable ground is:

- **Totton and the western fringe** (Totton, Marchwood, Hythe, Ashurst, Lyndhurst, Romsey): make the practice the default local result, not one of three.
- **Adviser and pension terms for Southampton and the New Forest**, where the map pack is held by firms with under 60 reviews and organic competitors are DR 3 to 24.
- **The informational demand the blog already attracts**, redirected into commercial pages with internal links and calls to action (the inherited-property post gets 25 visits a month and links nowhere useful).

| Framing | Assumptions | Annual first-year revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | Own Totton, one or two Southampton adviser terms on page one, 2 extra enquiries a month | ~£12k |
| Realistic | Above plus New Forest and Romsey adviser terms, map-pack presence for Southampton adviser searches, 4 a month, wealth mix rising | ~£32k to £40k |
| Stretch | Page-one for Southampton adviser terms and one mortgage long-tail cluster (self-employed, bad credit), 6 a month | ~£48k to £60k |

The bigger prize is the group: 28 advisers, 13 practices, at least one other practice already running its own branch site. A branch-site playbook proven at Totton is a repeatable product for head office (section 9).

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

This is not a textbook TenTerms client: the customer is a consumer, the practice is small, and the balance sheet is thin. It is, however, a business whose website badly underperforms the quality of its reviews and its local standing, with a short path to the decision and a real gap to close.

**Lead with a fixed-price local visibility project, not a retainer.** Suggested shape, priced in the £2k to £4k range:

1. Google Business Profile and review programme (19 reviews at 5.0 is the strongest asset they have; they need 60 to 100 to hold the Southampton map pack).
2. Rewrite the six service pages for Southampton and Totton intent, add fees or a fee explanation, embed reviews, and add two or three location pages (New Forest, Romsey, Eastleigh) if the group's compliance process allows.
3. Build a pensions and retirement advice page set for Nick's side, which is where the value per client is.
4. Fix the blog: internal links from the traffic-earning posts to the commercial pages, and stop publishing generic guides that do not rank.
5. Directory and listicle presence (VouchedFor, Unbiased, the "best broker in Southampton" round-ups) as the AI-visibility groundwork.

If the project lands and Lorna wants to continue, the lowest retainer tier is the right follow-on. The genuinely attractive commercial outcome is an introduction to head office to roll the same playbook across the group's practices, so the Totton project should be scoped as a case study from day one.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. Roughly how many new mortgage cases and new advice clients does the practice take on in a year, and what does a typical first-year fee look like for each? (Confirms section 7.)
2. What proportion of new business comes from referrals, BNI and existing clients versus people who found you online? (Confirms the cost of doing nothing.)
3. Nick, what does the practice look like in five years, and is the plan for Lorna to grow it or maintain it? (Tests headroom and who the decision really belongs to.)
4. Who writes and approves the blog now, and how does compliance sign-off with head office work for a new web page? (Tests capacity and lead time.)
5. Which side of the business would you rather grow: mortgages or pensions and investments? (Steers the plan to the higher-value line.)
6. Is there anyone at head office who would need to approve marketing spend or who might want to see results across other practices? (Decision authority and the group upsell.)
7. What happens when someone searches for a mortgage broker in Southampton today, in your experience, and does the practice want to compete for Southampton or own Totton and the New Forest? (Sets a realistic target.)
8. Do you ask clients for Google reviews systematically, and would you be willing to? (The cheapest lever on the table.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **Ability to pay.** NWI Consulting's cash has fallen from £53k to £17k in two years and net assets to £9k. Revenue is not disclosed. If the practice is drawing everything out, marketing budget will feel like a personal expense. Keep the entry small.
- **Consumer, not B2B.** Outside the core TenTerms pattern. Lead value per mortgage case is modest; the economics only work if the wealth side is in scope.
- **Compliance friction.** Every page is a financial promotion needing group sign-off. Turnaround could slow delivery; scope for it.
- **Existing content supplier.** The monthly generic blog posts suggest a current content or marketing provider. Establish who, and whether they also hold the site.
- **Succession.** Nick's age and the 2024 to 2026 share transfers to Lorna indicate a handover. This could cut either way: Lorna investing in her future, or Nick reluctant to spend on his exit.
- **Head office ownership of the brand.** The group may prefer a central site over branch sites. Check whether other branch sites (Petersfield) are group-sanctioned.
- **Not established:** actual turnover, fee structure, current enquiry volume, who built and hosts the site, and whether any AI assistant names the practice for local queries.
- **Credibility landmines.** None found. A 2020 strike-off notice against NWI Consulting was discontinued within a day (late filing), and all filings since are on time. Reviews are uniformly positive.
