## 3. What they do and who they sell to

Inspire, trading since 2005, is an ABTA (L4341), ATOL (11183) and IATA licensed agency and tour operator working with over 250 suppliers (TUI, Jet2holidays, Kuoni, Sandals and others). Four revenue lanes matter:

1. **TravelCard gift cards**: £5.8m sales in FY25, up 26 percent. Sold direct and through aggregators (Prezzee lists "Inspire Travel" on its travel gift card page). The board calls this the core growth product and has set up Inspire Germany GmbH to take it into Europe.
2. **Homeworking travel agents**: gross turnover £18.4m in FY25, up 61 percent (from £11.4m). Growth here depends partly on recruiting agents, which is itself a search market.
3. **Charity travel programmes**: partners include Family Fund, Kidney Care UK and Family Holiday Charity, served through white-label sites. The accounts say another partner is close to signing.
4. **Consumer holidays**: package and tailor-made travel via travelbyinspire.co.uk, with a call centre in Bramhall.

Search-strategy implications: national, not local; two distinct audiences (consumers buying holidays and gift cards; businesses and charities buying schemes); seasonal peaks in January; and an estate of at least four domains splitting whatever authority the brand earns.

## 4. Who's who

- **Lisa Henning** (CEO, director since August 2023, co-owner): the strategic report credits her personally with the homeworker division strategy. Likely the decision maker for growth spend.
- **Richard Buxton** (Finance Director, company secretary since 2020, co-owner): the other signature that matters; any retainer will cross his desk. Active on LinkedIn.
- **Ownership**: a December 2024 management buyout. New topco Inspiring Travel 2024 Limited (incorporated September 2024) is owned 25 to 50 percent each by Henning and Buxton, with Buxton also holding the right to appoint or remove directors. The previous generation (Peter Pantelides, Peter Main, Benjamin Ahrens) resigned as directors in April 2025; a new charge was registered in December 2024 and three old charges were satisfied in January 2025, consistent with acquisition funding.
- **Delivery contact**: none identified by name, but the About page describes in-house web developers, product and marketing specialists, so a delivery contact should exist. Confirm at discovery.
- **Intermediary risk**: low if we reach either owner, because the two people who can say yes are the CEO and the FD. The problem is reachability, not authority; this is a cold prospect until a route is found.

Reading: strong top-line growth with margin compression (gross margin fell from 10.8 to 8.8 percent as competition and cost-of-living pressures bit; an Egypt client programme was also paused). **Affordability**: a £1,500 to £4,500 monthly retainer is comfortably affordable against £3.4m gross profit and £3.8m cash. The nuance is that post-MBO owners watching PBT fall 28 percent will want spend tied visibly to the two divisions they have publicly bet on: gift cards and homeworkers. Growth mode, but disciplined.

## 6. Search and AI visibility snapshot

*Spot check from public data (Ahrefs, live Google via SERPAPI), 29 July 2026. Not a full audit.*

**Where they are now.** travelbyinspire.co.uk: Domain Rating 42, roughly 5,000 organic visits a month, 265 ranking keywords in the UK, no paid search. Traffic has been flat between about 4,000 and 6,600 a month for three years. Nearly all of it is brand: "inspire", "inspire holidays", "inspire travel card" and variants. Best non-brand positions are "trips to italy" at 11, "cheap disneyland paris holidays" at 12 and "travel agents" at 22. The corporate site inspirewebsite.co.uk is effectively invisible: 35 keywords, about 110 visits a month. A £38.8m business is being found online only by people who already know its name.

Volumes are modest per term but each sits atop a long-tail cluster, difficulty is low, and Inspire's DR 42 outguns several sites that do rank. Consumer holiday head terms ("holidays to spain" and the like) are owned by TUI, Jet2 and the OTAs and should not be fought.

**Competitor set for the gift card SERP**: One4all, Prezzee, giftcards.co.uk, travelgift.uk, Trailfinders, lastminute.com, TUI, Asda Gift Cards. Retail gift-card players, not travel agents; content is thin category pages.

**AI answer spot check**: no AI Overview appeared on the two queries checked. The comparison content AI answers draw from (Guardian voucher round-up, Reddit, Mumsnet, Giftronaut's explainer) largely omits Inspire. The one exception is telling: the number 2 result for "best travel gift card uk" is a Reddit thread in which a user volunteers that "the travel card by Inspire looks good". Third-party advocacy exists; nothing on their own estate captures it. Run two or three live assistant prompts before any call if AI visibility will feature.

**What a buyer sees.** Someone searching "travel gift card uk" sees One4all, Prezzee, two gift-card marketplaces, Trailfinders, lastminute.com and TUI. Inspire, whose gift card sold £5.8m last year and which Prezzee itself stocks, appears only inside a Reddit comment. A charity or HR manager searching "corporate travel incentives" or "employee reward schemes" finds no trace of Inspire on either of its sites.

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

Margin discipline first: Inspire keeps roughly 8.8p of gross profit per £1 booked, so all value below is stated in gross profit (GP), not booking turnover.

- **Consumer booking or gift card redemption**: a £2,000 to £3,000 booking yields roughly £175 to £265 GP. A volume lane.
- **Corporate, charity or scheme partner**: one partner generates a programme of bookings. On their own numbers, three charity partners sit within a business doing £38.8m, and the strategic report treats each new partner signing as board-level news. A modest scheme running £0.5m to £2m of bookings a year would be worth roughly £45k to £175k GP annually, with multi-year retention. Assumption: scheme partners book at blended margin; confirm at discovery.
- **Recruited homeworking agent**: the division grew £7m in one year; each recruited agent carries a book of bookings. "Become a travel agent from home" costs almost nothing to win (KD 1) and feeds this directly.

Conversion chain on the B2B lane, assuming a mid-case £25k first-year GP per scheme partner and conservative close rates:

| Scenario | Extra qualified B2B enquiries/month | Close rate | New partners/year | GP impact (first 12 months per partner) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 1 | 1 in 5 | ~2 | ~£50k |
| Realistic | 2 | 1 in 4 | ~6 | ~£150k |
| Stretch | 3 | 1 in 3 | ~12 | ~£300k |

(First-year value only; scheme and charity partners appear to retain for years, which is upside.) Adding the consumer lane: capturing even 300 extra gift-card-cluster visits a month at a 3 percent conversion is roughly 108 bookings a year, another £20k or so GP.

Against a mid-tier retainer of £3,000 a month (£36k a year), the conservative combined case (£70k GP) covers about 2x, short of the 3x comfort bar; the realistic case clears it easily. Honest conclusion: the ROI case leans on landing at least one scheme partner via search, so the pitch should be anchored on the B2B lane, with the gift card cluster as the supporting volume story.

## 8. Size of the prize

- **Conservative**: own the winnable gift card cluster plus homeworker recruitment terms, generate one scheme enquiry a month; roughly £70k GP a year. Assumes no movement on consumer head terms.
- **Realistic**: add the employee rewards and corporate incentive cluster on a rebuilt B2B presence, two scheme enquiries a month, and Reddit-style third-party advocacy converted into owned comparison content that AI answers can cite; £150k to £200k GP a year.
- **Stretch**: category ownership of "travel gift card" in the UK plus a German replay of the same playbook as Inspire Germany GmbH launches; £300k+ GP a year. Assumes multi-market scope and board appetite.

The strategic frame for the call: the board has publicly committed (in filed accounts) to growing exactly the two divisions that search currently sends them nothing for.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

Fit is good but non-standard: bigger than the usual ICP, with a B2C flank we should mostly leave alone. The underperformance pattern, however, is textbook, including a four-domain estate splitting the brand's signals (consumer site, corporate site, sustainability site, charity white-labels) and a corporate site that is invisible for terms its own accounts describe as strategic.

**Entry point: a paid visibility review**, scoped to (1) the TravelCard and gift card cluster, (2) the B2B rewards and incentives presence including the estate question, before proposing consolidation, and (3) homeworker recruitment terms. Lead with the buyer-experience paragraph in section 6 and the accounts-versus-search contrast; both owners will recognise their own strategy document. A retainer pitch comes after the review, sized mid-tier.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. Of the £5.8m gift card sales, how much comes through your own site versus partners like Prezzee, and what is the margin difference on a direct sale? (Tests the gift card GP assumption.)
2. What is a charity or corporate scheme partner worth to you in bookings in a typical year, and how did the last one find you? (Tests the £25k GP per partner assumption.)
3. How many homeworking agents do you have now, what is the recruitment target, and what does it currently cost to recruit one? (Tests the recruitment lane value.)
4. The accounts mention another charity partner close to signing: where do new scheme conversations come from today, and how many are you in at once? (Sizes the enquiry pipeline search would need to add to.)
5. Who internally would own an SEO programme day to day, given you have in-house developers and marketing specialists? (Capacity.)
6. If the numbers stacked up, would this be a decision you and Richard take together, or does anything else need to happen first? (Authority.)
7. With Germany launching, is UK visibility this year's priority or next year's? (Budget timing.)
8. Is any agency currently doing SEO or content for either site? (Incumbent risk.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **No warm route**: cold outreach to a post-MBO board; Decision Reachability is the weakest score. Buxton is active on LinkedIn, which is the likeliest door.
- **Scale mismatch**: larger than the typical TenTerms client; they may default to a big-agency shortlist. Counter with specificity of the review findings.
- **Thin margin economics**: any proposal quoting booking turnover instead of gross profit will overstate the prize about elevenfold and cost credibility.
- **Post-MBO cash discipline**: PBT down 28 percent, cash down £1.2m, £1.6m lent upwards to the parent. Affordable, but expect FD-grade scrutiny.
- **Unwinnable B2C head terms**: the consumer holiday market is owned by nationals; scope must exclude it explicitly or the engagement will be judged against it.
- **Estate complexity**: four domains plus white-labels; consolidation recommendations touch partner-facing infrastructure and will need careful staging.
- **Reputation**: Trustpilot 4.1 from 2,105 reviews is solid but not spotless; not a blocker, worth reading the recent negatives before the call.
- **Unverified**: LinkedIn headcount versus the 48 in the accounts; the FY24 accounts document itself (server error on fetch; FY25 restated comparatives used); current Google Business Profile review counts; live AI assistant answers (run two or three prompts before the call).
