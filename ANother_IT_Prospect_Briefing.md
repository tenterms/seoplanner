## 3. What they do and who they sell to

ANother is a classic small MSP. Core recurring services: managed IT support (remote and on-site), cyber security and compliance, Microsoft 365 and cloud, backup and disaster recovery, and strategic "vCIO" IT leadership. Around that sit project and hardware lines: procurement and installation, desktop and server migrations, structured cabling and wireless, VoIP, CCTV and AV, and photocopier supply. Thirteen dedicated service pages plus a services hub, and at least some town-level location pages (Alton, Farnborough confirmed).

Customers are SMEs across Hampshire, Surrey and London; testimonials name Hawk Conservancy Trust, Upping Your Elvis, Planet Languages and PMW Dynamics, so charity, consultancy, language services and engineering. Search strategy is local/regional service coverage: "[IT service] [town]" terms across the GU-postcode belt and the wider counties. One service line (photocopiers) and one audience (charities) could support their own clusters later.

The business is young on paper (August 2024) but not in substance: Aaron Nother previously ran the Farnham office of Saqqara IT, and a Financial Profiles testimonial refers to working with him for over 25 years. This looks like an experienced operator spinning out with a carried client base, which explains the healthy first-year balance sheet.

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

One new managed IT client for a firm this size is typically worth **£8k–£20k a year** (10–25 users at roughly £40–£80 per user per month, plus projects and hardware), and MSP clients commonly stay three years or more. Assumption basis: sector norms; Coreitech's advertised £25/user/month marks the local price floor, and ANother's positioning (vCIO, compliance) suggests mid-market rather than floor pricing. No published pricing on their site to anchor against — a discovery question.

Conversion chain, using first-12-months value only:

| Scenario | Extra enquiries/month | Close rate | New clients/year | Revenue impact (first 12 months per client) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 1 | 1 in 5 | ~2 | £16k–£40k |
| Realistic | 2 | 1 in 4 | ~6 | £48k–£120k |
| Stretch | 3 | 1 in 3 | ~12 | £96k–£240k |

(Lifetime value is 3x+ these figures given typical MSP retention; kept out of the headline deliberately.)

Against a £1,500/month retainer (£18k/year): the conservative case roughly breaks even in year one and pays 3x+ over client lifetime; the realistic case clears 3x in year one. Against £4,500/month the maths only works at stretch. Conclusion: the value case supports the **lower retainer tier or a project entry**, not the top tier.

## 8. Size of the prize

The immediately addressable cluster (the table in §6, excluding the national "outsourced it support" term) is roughly 1,800 local searches a month with difficulty scores of 0–4 and competitors whose main moat is longevity messaging and review counts, not content quality. ANother already has the page architecture; the pages need to actually compete (content depth, titles, reviews, claimed GBP, local links).

- **Conservative:** top-3 positions on Alton, Farnborough and Farnham terms only (their two home towns plus the confirmed ranking) → 1–2 qualified enquiries a month → 2–4 new clients a year → **£16k–£80k of first-year recurring revenue**.
- **Realistic:** add Hampshire, Basingstoke and "managed it services hampshire" top-5 → 2–3 enquiries a month → **£48k–£120k**.
- **Stretch:** compete on Surrey/Guildford and take a share of "outsourced it support" (1,300/month, difficulty 0) with a strong hub page → **£100k+** and a step-change in inbound.

Each new client also compounds: MSP contracts renew, so year-two prize is prior wins retained plus new wins.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

Strong fit on pattern (managed IT is a named good-fit sector), weak only on scale. Recommended entry:

1. **Lead with the paid visibility review**, using the Farnham SERP evidence above as the teaser. It prices the relationship correctly for a first-year company and produces the roadmap.
2. **Follow with the £1,500 tier retainer** focused on: claiming and building out the Google Business Profile and reviews engine; rewriting the existing location and service pages to actually compete (merchandised titles, real local substance); a location-page build-out across the GU belt (Guildford, Basingstoke, Aldershot, Winchester); and an "outsourced IT support" hub as the national swing.
3. Quick win to offer in the first conversation as a good-faith gift: **the GBP is unclaimed** — claiming it costs nothing and they should do it this week regardless of who they hire.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. What is a typical client worth to you per year, and how long do clients stay? (Replaces the £8k–£20k assumption.)
2. Where do new clients come from today — how much is referral and networking, and what happened the last time a stranger found you online? (Tests whether the invisibility is felt as a cost.)
3. How many new clients could you take on in the next 12 months before hiring? (Sizes the realistic scenario and the delivery ceiling.)
4. You rank for almost nothing despite having the right pages — did an agency build the site, and is anyone responsible for it now? (Surfaces incumbent/DIY history.)
5. Who besides you would look at the numbers before you committed to a monthly retainer — does Pepita weigh in on spend? (Decision authority check.)
6. If we needed a couple of hours a month from your side to review pages and gather client reviews, who would that be? (Capacity check.)
7. Did you know your Google Business Profile is unclaimed? (Instant credibility, zero-cost win.)
8. Are the project lines (CCTV, photocopiers, cabling) businesses you want more of, or add-ons for support clients? (Scopes the keyword universe before anyone builds it.)

## 11. Risks and open items

- **Turnover is estimated, not known.** First filleted accounts only; the £500k–£1m band rests on the headcount heuristic. Confirm affordability in conversation.
- **No marketing function.** Delivery depends on the founder's diary; a stalled-approval risk. Mitigate by scoping light-touch review cycles.
- **Referral-led culture.** 25 years of relationships may mean inbound search has never felt necessary; the pitch must price the invisible loss, not sell SEO in the abstract.
- **Young entity.** One year of filings; no trend data. Nothing negative on file (accounts and confirmation statement both on time).
- **Website footer omits the company number** (checked the homepage) — minor, but a trust-signal and distance-selling compliance point that also weakens entity signals for AI answers.
- **Saqqara spin-out dynamics unknown.** Any restrictive covenants or sensitivities about competing in Farnham are worth understanding before writing town-page copy.
- Source of this lead not recorded — if it is not inbound, reachability drops a notch until Aaron is in a conversation.
