## 3. What they do and who they sell to

Greenpoint is a managed service provider founded in 1999. Core lines: managed IT support, connectivity (broadband and leased lines), disaster recovery, GDPR and security consultancy, hosted telephony, Microsoft 365/Azure/SharePoint/Power Platform work, and digital transformation projects. They also run a secondary web services arm (design, hosting, digital marketing, SEO, PPC).

Customers are UK SMEs, sold on a relationship-led, "partners not suppliers" positioning. Two offices give them two local markets: the Farnborough/Camberley/Aldershot corridor on the Hampshire/Surrey border, and Worthing in West Sussex. Search strategy is local and regional rather than national, with a services-led architecture already in place (the site has location pages for Farnborough and Camberley, which currently do not rank).

## 7. What a new customer is worth to them

A managed IT client for a 15-person MSP is typically worth £10k to £40k a year; taking a mid-range SME contract, one new client is conservatively worth about £15k in its first 12 months, and MSP clients commonly stay three years or more (retention is upside, not headline). Close rate assumed at 1 in 5 to 1 in 3 for inbound, high-intent enquiries.

| Scenario | Extra enquiries/month | Close rate | New clients/year | Revenue impact (first 12 months per client, at £15k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 2 | 1 in 5 | ~5 | ~£75k |
| Realistic | 4 | 1 in 4 | ~12 | ~£180k |
| Stretch | 6 | 1 in 3 | ~24 | ~£360k |

The conservative case is roughly 3x the top-tier annual retainer (£54k) and over 4x a mid-tier one, before any retention effect. The ROI case clears the bar without stretching.

## 8. Size of the prize

Around 1,500+ commercial searches a month across their two geographies at near-zero difficulty, against a current baseline of essentially nil non-brand traffic. If Greenpoint captured even a modest share of the Farnborough/Camberley/Hampshire cluster plus Worthing, 2 to 4 qualified enquiries a month is a realistic 6-to-12-month outcome, worth £75k to £180k a year in new managed services revenue on the assumptions above. The stretch case adds the "managed it services" and Microsoft-specialism terms, where their genuine Power Platform and SharePoint capability gives them content few local rivals can write. Every figure above carries its stated assumption; their real average contract value should replace the £15k estimate after discovery.

## 9. TenTerms fit and suggested entry point

Strong fit: B2B, high customer value, search-driven local market, a good business with a website that badly underperforms it, and comfortable ability to pay. Suggested entry: lead with a **paid visibility review** rather than a straight retainer pitch. Two reasons. First, the evidence is unusually vivid here (27 years in Farnborough, invisible for "IT support Farnborough", unclaimed Google listing) and a review turns that into an undeniable document. Second, Greenpoint sells SEO themselves, so a retainer pitch invites "we do this in-house"; a review reframes the conversation around results, not services. Quick wins to name in the room: claim the Google Business Profile, build a review-generation habit, and rebuild the existing location pages that already exist but do not rank.

## 10. Discovery questions

1. What does an average managed services client pay you a year, and how long do they typically stay? (Replaces the £15k assumption.)
2. Where did your last five new clients come from? What share was referral versus anything the website produced?
3. Roughly how many enquiries does the website generate a month today, and what happens to them?
4. Your site has IT Support Farnborough and Camberley pages that don't rank; who built them and who looks after the site now? (Tests capacity and surfaces the in-house web team dynamic.)
5. You offer SEO to your own clients; how do you think about buying it in for yourselves? (Surfaces the conflict early, on our terms.)
6. If the website brought in three qualified enquiries a month, what would that be worth to you, and what would stop you serving them? (Cost of doing nothing plus capacity.)
7. Beyond you, who would want to see the numbers before going ahead? Caroline? (Tests decision authority politely.)
8. Your Google listing is unclaimed and has 7 reviews against a competitor's 61; is there a reason reviews haven't been a focus?

## 11. Risks and open items

- **They sell SEO.** The web services arm lists SEO and digital marketing. Risk: "we do it ourselves" objection, or pride. Opportunity: they clearly don't apply it to their own site, and a white-label or referral relationship could be a second prize. Handle with care; do not open by criticising their own service line.
- **Lead source unknown.** Scored as cold; reachability improves sharply if there is a warm route to Alex Green.
- **Stable rather than growth-mode.** Flat headcount and fully distributed profits may mean Alex is comfortable at current size, or eyeing retirement (b. 1967). If there is no appetite for growth, the value case weakens; question 6 tests this.
- **Turnover is estimated**, not disclosed; the £1.5m to £3m band rests on the 15-employee figure and MSP norms.
- **AI answer check was indicative only**; run live prompts before the call.
- No distress signals: filings on time, no charges, no adverse reviews found. The dormant GPOINT LIMITED is unremarkable name protection.
