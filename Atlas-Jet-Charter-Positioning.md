## 1. Customer Profile

### Our customer is…

A high-net-worth individual — or the executive assistant, family office or concierge booking on their behalf — who flies privately for efficiency, privacy and reliability, and who has been burned (or expects to be) by an industry "known for smoke-and-mirrors" (client). They are wealth managers, hedge-fund principals, CEOs of large conglomerates, Knightsbridge clientele, and the professional gatekeepers who serve them.

Two distinct buying modes:

1. **On-demand charterer** — flies occasionally or seasonally (Riviera summers, Geneva winters, transatlantic business), wants a flawless flight arranged around their diary with zero friction.
2. **Frequent flyer / membership prospect** — flies 10–25+ hours a year, wants charter-market flexibility with ownership-like price stability (capped hourly rates, guaranteed availability). The site targets "entrepreneurs, executives, and frequent private flyers seeking stable pricing and availability."

### Their primary desire (website-ready)

*"A private aviation partner I can genuinely trust — one who knows every operator, tells me exactly what I'm paying for, and makes every journey effortless from my first call to my front door."*

### What they don't want

- Opaque, padded quotes and a "convoluted" process engineered "to generate more margin" (client)
- Being one of hundreds of accounts at a high-volume brokerage — handed between call-centre staff
- Uncertainty about who is actually operating the aircraft, and whether it's safe
- Price-led brokers competing to the bottom on quality
- Surprises: hidden fees, peak-day surcharges, expiring hours, blackout dates
- Wasting their own (extremely valuable) time chasing updates or managing logistics

### Their dream outcome

Private flight that is simply *handled*. They message one trusted specialist — often a founder — and everything else happens: the right vetted aircraft, a transparent all-in price, ground transport, catering, the helicopter for the last leg, the villa on arrival. They never think about the mechanics again, and the more they fly, the better the economics get.

## 2. Problem Analysis

### Real-world problems (literal)

- The charter market is fragmented and opaque: anyone can become a broker and resell flights with no expertise ("anyone can join a brokerage and sell flights" — client)
- Pricing is deliberately convoluted to hide margin; clients can't compare quotes meaningfully
- Safety is uneven: "some operators / aircraft combinations are unsafe — we wouldn't fly on it" (client)
- Many brokers serve Europe/US well but fail on the Far East, Australia, Central & South America (client)
- Practical ground knowledge is missing — brokers who don't know "where there's no parking when you land" (client)

### Obstacles to success (for the customer)

- No reliable way to verify a broker's claims (accreditations, operator vetting, insurance)
- Time poverty: they cannot personally research operators, AOCs and maintenance records
- Gatekeepers (EAs, concierges) carry personal reputational risk for every booking they place
- Frequent flyers face volatile spot pricing and peak-day availability crunches

### Emotional impact

- **Distrust** — "they don't know who they can trust, so finding someone they *can* trust is very important" (client)
- **Anxiety** about safety on unfamiliar operators, especially with family aboard
- **Irritation** at being treated as a transaction by volume brokerages
- **Embarrassment risk** for EAs/concierges if a booked flight goes wrong

### Major frustrations (voice of customer)

- "What am I actually paying for in this quote?"
- "Who is really operating this aircraft — and would *you* fly on it?"
- "Why do I have a different account manager every time I call?"
- "Why does my hourly rate change every time I book?"
- "I don't have time to project-manage my own flight."

## 4. Motivation Framework

The four motivators that fit Atlas best: **Security (trust & safety), Time, Status, Comfort/Convenience** — with **Money** as a supporting motivator for the membership product specifically (capped rates, banked savings, empty legs at 20–70% off).

### SECURITY — the lead motivator

*Trigger questions*
- "Do you actually know who's operating your next charter flight — and would your broker fly on it themselves?"
- "What's really inside that quote you just signed?"

*Outcome statements*
- Every Atlas flight is on a vetted operator: valid AOC and insurance before a booking can even be generated, third-party accreditation (ARGUS, Wyvern, IS-BAO) or an equivalent internal audit, crew and maintenance records reviewed.
- One all-inclusive price, itemised before you sign. The only post-flight variable is de-icing — and we tell you that upfront.

*Visualisation prompt*
- "Picture boarding with your family knowing the operator, the crew pairing and the maintenance history were checked before the aircraft was ever offered to you."

*Trigger questions*
- "How many hours did your last trip cost you before you even took off?"

*Visualisation prompt*

*Visualisation prompt*

### Objection handling

**"You're not the cheapest quote I've had."**
Correct — and deliberately so (client: "not the cheapest"). The cheapest quote in this market usually hides its margin in the process or its risk in the operator. Atlas quotes are all-inclusive and itemised, on aircraft we'd fly ourselves. Cost-conscious flexibility exists too: empty legs at 20–70% off.

**"I already have a broker."**
Ask them three questions: who operates your next flight, what's their safety record, and what exactly is in your price? If the answers aren't immediate, that's the difference. Try Atlas on one trip and compare.

**"Why not book direct with an operator?"**
No single operator is the right answer for every route, date and aircraft type. Atlas sources across the whole vetted market in real time — and holds operators to a rating standard a one-off customer can't.

**"Membership feels like lock-in."**
Hours never expire, there are no blackout dates, rates are capped not floating, and if we source below your cap the savings are banked back to you. It's the opposite of the fractional/ownership lock-ins it replaces. (Site: membership "value becomes clear the more you fly"; on-demand remains "the cheapest way to fly" for occasional travel — and Atlas offers both.)

**(EA/concierge-specific) "If this goes wrong, it's on me."**
Referral relationships with concierges are Atlas's core channel (5–25% agreements). A named specialist owns the booking end-to-end, 24/7, and the vetting file behind every aircraft is available on request.

### Guarantees

- Guaranteed availability with 72 hours' notice (membership)
- Capped hourly rate — "rates never exceed your cap, regardless of market fluctuations"
- Hours never expire; no blackout dates; no peak-day pricing (membership)
- All-inclusive quotes with "no hidden fees"; any extras agreed before contract signature; the only post-flight variable is de-icing, disclosed upfront
- Client funds held in UK accounts
- Fair, negotiated cancellation and amendment terms
